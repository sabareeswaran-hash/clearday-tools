CLEAR ANSWERS, STEP BY STEP

Practical calculation guides

These short guides show the arithmetic behind common everyday questions. Use the examples as a check, then try your own numbers with the calculators on our home page.

How to find a percentage of a number

To find a percentage of an amount, convert the percentage to a decimal by dividing it by 100, then multiply by the amount.

Example: 15% of 80
15 ÷ 100 = 0.15
0.15 × 80 = 12

This works because “percent” means “per hundred.” So 15% is another way of writing 15 out of every 100, or 0.15 as a decimal.

How to calculate a sale discount

A discount is a percentage of the original price. First calculate the saving, then subtract it from the original price. You can also multiply the price by the part that remains after the discount.

Example: 20% off $50
Saving: $50 × 0.20 = $10
Sale price: $50 − $10 = $40

A 20% discount leaves 80% of the price to pay, so $50 × 0.80 also gives $40. Check whether a listed price includes taxes, delivery, or other charges, as those may be calculated separately.

How to work out percentage change

Percentage change compares the difference between an old value and a new value with the old value. Divide the difference by the old value, then multiply by 100.

Example: a price rises from 40 to 50
Change: 50 − 40 = 10
Percentage change: (10 ÷ 40) × 100 = 25% increase

For a decrease, the new value is smaller than the old value; describe the result as a percentage decrease. The old value is the baseline in both cases. If the old value is zero, percentage change is undefined because division by zero is not possible.

These examples explain basic arithmetic and are for general information. They are not financial, tax, or professional advice.
